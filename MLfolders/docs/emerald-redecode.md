# Emerald's batch, re-decoded with its compiled readout window

Generated by `scripts/emerald_redecode.py doc` from `InfoRecord/emerald/ler/summary/redecode_selection.json` (written by `select`, committed before any B3 number) and `redecode_report.json` (`report`). **fallback** is the batch's prior, `hardware_noise (79092cf)`: `hardware_noise(cal, spec, windows=None)`, the 360 ns `measure_ns` window. **compiled** is `hardware_noise compiled-1036ns (53b2a19)`: `hardware_noise(cal, spec)` reading `data/calibration/emerald/pulse/compile.23e12f6b-….json`, 1,036 ns on every eA and eB ancilla. Everything else in the prior is the same. All intervals are 95%: Wilson for P_L, a seeded bootstrap (1000, seed 2026) for ε.

## The rebuild

Before either prior was trusted, every one of the 112 B1+B2 runs (`select`) and then all 156 runs of B1–B3 (`report`) were rebuilt from the committed raw memory: the native circuit equals `placement.json`'s, `memory.npz` equals `raw_meas.npy`, the detection events and observable equal the dataset's, the split equals `suite.split`, and the fallback prior gives back the dataset's decoder note, its DEM size and its per-shot `pm_fail` exactly (so every committed failure count). The fallback model's detection fraction (seed 2026, 20,000 shots) equals `batch_checks.json`'s on 156 of 156 runs. The pilot EP0 is not part of the batch and is not re-decoded here (`docs/pulla-check.md` has it).

## What it says

- **The compiled window decodes worse, so the batch's prior stays.** On finetune validation the compiled prior fails more often on eA_Z, eB_Z, eB_X (McNemar p < 0.05), and on eA_X 1938 against 1937 (p 1.00); the rule chooses fallback on 4 of 4. B3 agrees after the fact: compiled ε (A = 1) is eA Z 0.0504 against 0.0476, eA X 0.0374 against 0.0366, eB Z 0.0477 against 0.0448, eB X 0.0477 against 0.0431. The headline B3 numbers of `docs/batches.md` therefore stand.
- **It fits the detection statistics far better.** Over every E1 circuit the hardware's detection fraction is eA Z 1.17–1.75× the fallback model and 0.90–1.30× the compiled one; eA X 0.87–1.61× the fallback model and 0.85–1.13× the compiled one; eB Z 1.23–1.52× the fallback model and 0.95–1.31× the compiled one; eB X 1.08–1.57× the fallback model and 0.88–1.16× the compiled one. The bulk rounds land near 1; round 1 (t = 0, no idle before it) stays at 1.35–1.42× in the Z basis, and the last rounds of long circuits stay above 1. A better-matched model that decodes worse is garnet-timing's `b_pauli` story again: the decoder needs the right relative weights, not the right totals.
- **The fit form matters more than the prior.** The first-round model wins on all four finetune pools; on B3 it raises ε by 17–22% over the challenge's A = 1 form (amplitude 1.07–1.12), well outside the bootstrap intervals.
- **X against Z.** eA: X < Z under both priors and every pool (ε_X/ε_Z 0.74–0.81, z -3.4 to -2.7 inflated), against a model X/Z of 0.98 (fallback) and 0.94 (compiled): a real excess of the errors a Z-basis memory sees. eB: 0.96 with fallback and 1.00 compiled on B3, |z| ≤ 0.5, no round count separates, and the model itself gives 0.90 / 0.95: not established. 'Bit-flip-heavy' holds for eA only.
- **QB10** reads 1 on 0.413 of its random round-1 readouts (asymmetry ≈ 0.17, against a calibration error of 0.0156). Giving the decoder that readout error on B1+B2 changes nothing in Z and 10045 → 9984 in X (p 0.46): it doesn't move eB's numbers.

## eB's QB10 (MINOR 5)

- **Size.** QB10 is an X-type ancilla of eB. In the Z basis its round-1 readout is random, and it reads 1 on 0.413 of shots (B1–B3 pooled), against 0.468–0.523 for eB's other X-type ancillas. If the projection is 50/50, that is P(0|1) − P(1|0) ≈ 0.174. In the X basis its round-1 readout is deterministic, and it misreads 0.0922. The calibration has P(1|0) 0.0126, P(0|1) 0.0156, T1 45.8 µs: the bias is not in the calibration.

Without reset, an X-type ancilla in the Z basis reads s₁ (random) in odd rounds: those stay below 0.5 in every round, so the bias persists. The X-basis misread includes the stabilizer's gate errors, so it bounds P(1|0) from above, and the sensitivity flip below is an upper-end value.
- **Does it move eB?** Sensitivity on B1+B2 only (finetune side), never on B3: QB10's record flip set to (P(0|1) + P(1|0)) / 2 from the raw memory, on every readout of QB10. Record flip 0.1793:
